## The excluded round

k=2, round 1: the ramp stopped at 3456 pps offered, but the per-second delivered rates inside that step were mostly at the offered rate with a stall of about two seconds in the middle (see `results.json`, step `binsPps`). A capacity limit would hold the delivered rate down for the whole step. The same k in round 2 saturated cleanly at 10951 pps. It resembles the unexplained stall recorded in the 2026-10-04 ladder at low load. The classification rule (a saturated step is a stall if at least half of its interior seconds delivered at the offered rate) was written after seeing this step and is stated here for that reason; the slope above does not depend on it for k=0, 4 and 8, which have two clean rounds each.

## What this says, and does not

- The decision (A4 wins: quarantines above all forwarding rules) is **not contradicted** by the hardware: the measured capacities are monotonic in k and reproducible between rounds. But the **cost estimate in the decision record was too low**: see the slope and the prediction row above.
- The fixed cost of the deployed policy is higher than the two-rule model, as expected (more rules scanned ahead of the matching one); this run does not separate that from the per-quarantine slope.
- Not measured: the forwarding-first layout (the quarantine path cannot build it), a frame matched by a different rule than the tap-only rule, TCP, switch CPU, firmware other than 0.86. One switch.
